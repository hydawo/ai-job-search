# Venture Capital: Priority Long-Term Career Target

**Status:** Confirmed 2026-08-21 as Hassan's fastest, most-guaranteed route to his $500K comp goal — faster than the operator/VP/Director/CPO product-leadership track on its own. Upgraded from "secondary, ~10 years out" to an active parallel objective: the primary digital-health product/strategy search continues unchanged, but it now runs alongside an active search for VC and VC-stepping-stone roles, regardless of whether those roles involve product management at all.

## Why this is realistic, not aspirational

Hassan's profile doesn't match the classic VC-associate pipeline (banking/consulting into a fund), but it matches a different, real entry point: **domain-expert operator** in a niche (digital phenotyping, wearables, digital biomarkers, regulated clinical data) that generalist VCs and even many healthcare VCs cannot technically diligence on their own. That expertise is the asset — the path is built around monetizing it into VC access, not around acquiring a finance pedigree from scratch.

## Fastest lane, re-ranked 2026-08-21: direct vertical-fund hire

The CVC-via-product-role and consulting-to-VC lanes below both require 18 months to several years in an *intermediate* job before the VC move happens. That's not actually the fastest path available today. The fastest path is to **skip the intermediate job and target thematic/vertical healthcare VC funds directly, pitched as a domain-expert hire, not a generalist**.

Rationale: vertical funds (Rock Health, Define Ventures, .406 Ventures, Oak HC/FT, and similar) hire Principals/Venture Partners/Platform-team people specifically because they need someone who can technically diligence digital phenotyping/passive-sensing/wearable-data claims that they cannot evaluate in-house — a gap Hassan already fills today, not one he's years from closing. These funds screen less on banking pedigree than generalist funds do, because the job is technical diligence and portfolio support, not sourcing proprietary deal flow from a GP network.

**Concrete sequence, fastest first:**
1. **Now, no job change:** pursue a scout or EIR program at one of these funds — lowest-friction entry, and the actual credential check funds use before a full-time invite (see "What to build now" below).
2. **In parallel, actively search and apply to Principal/Platform/vertical-expert openings at these same funds directly** — do not wait for a CVC-adjacent product role to season for 18-24 months first. This is a direct shot at the goal, not a detour through operator status.
3. Treat the CVC and consulting lanes below as fallback/parallel-optionality lanes, not the primary route — they're more certain per-attempt but slower.

**Search instruction (added 2026-08-21):** alongside the primary digital-health product/strategy search, actively search for and surface: (a) openings at healthtech-focused VC funds themselves (Principal, Venture Partner, Platform, EIR, scout — any seniority), (b) roles at any employer that are explicit stepping stones toward VC per the three on-ramp lanes below, regardless of whether the role has anything to do with product management. A role's relevance to this goal is not gated on product-management content.

## The other on-ramps, in order of certainty (not speed)

### A. Domain-expert / corporate VC (CVC) path — slower, but a strong fallback
Corporate venture arms hire for domain fluency over deal-sheet pedigree, because the job is technical diligence and portfolio support, not sourcing proprietary deal flow from a GP network.

- **CVS Health Ventures** — already on the target-company list (`CLAUDE.md`). A Director/Principal-level role there, or a healthcare-strategy role that feeds into it, is the most direct lane available today.
- **Sanofi Ventures / Amgen Ventures / Takeda Ventures** — if Hassan lands a digital health/digital biomarker product role at Sanofi, Takeda, or Amgen, pursue internal mobility into the venture arm after ~18-24 months. Pharma CVCs need people who can technically diligence digital phenotyping/wearable-data startups — this is exactly Hassan's niche.
- **Verily** — closer to GV and the broader Alphabet/health VC network than Oura or WHOOP; worth weighting slightly higher for this specific goal if choosing among comparable offers.

### B. Consulting-to-VC pipeline — parallel, builds optionality
McKinsey / BCG / KPMG healthcare & life-sciences practices are a well-trodden route into healthcare-focused VC; funds actively recruit ex-consultants with commercial-diligence experience. If one of these firms becomes a realistic option, weight it up — 2-3 years of healthcare diligence work there is a stronger VC resume line than another straight product role.

### C. Generalist strategy/ops or finance role at an investor-backed startup — surfaced, but weaker and slower than A/B
This is the closest analog to the **traditional finance-to-VC path** (banking/consulting-analyst-style generalist roles), except the pedigree signal comes from company brand and investor quality rather than a bank/consulting-firm name. A rotational strategy/ops, corp-dev, or FP&A-adjacent seat at a well-regarded, well-funded startup (health-tech or otherwise) can plausibly feed into VC recruiting the way an analyst program does, but it's a less-traveled, lower-certainty route than the CVC or consulting lanes above — evaluated case-by-case, weighted heavily by the specific company's investor caliber and whether the role is described as a springboard/rotational program versus a permanent generalist seat.

- Example evaluated 2026-08-21: Garner Health's "Senior Strategy & Operations Manager" (NYC, onsite — failed on location, not on this criterion). The posting explicitly screens for consulting/IB/early-stage-startup backgrounds and describes rotating through high-impact initiatives — structurally similar to a strategy-rotation program, which is the shape of role this lane is looking for, even though it didn't ultimately clear the location gate.
- Surfacing rule: flag roles in this category even when they don't fit the primary digital-health-product search criteria, since the point is optionality toward VC specifically, not product-career progression.

**Added to job-evaluation criteria:** `04-job-evaluation.md` now flags postings as "VC-track" when they match a corporate VC arm, a healthcare-consulting diligence practice, an explicitly investment/venture-facing title (venture partner, EIR, scout, portfolio strategy), **or** a generalist strategy/ops/finance role at a well-funded, investor-backed startup structured as a rotational or springboard program — noted in the evaluation output, not scored into the numeric fit.

## How this nests with the primary VP/Director/CPO path

Not competing tracks — sequential leverage. A CPO or VP Product title at a recognized digital health company (Oura, Verily, a notable pharma digital health unit) makes Hassan *more* attractive later as a venture partner or EIR, because funds want operators who've shipped and scaled, not just diligenced. "Operator with a strong outcome becomes venture partner" is the highest-status entry but the slowest — realistically 5-8+ years out, likely after a CPO-level stint. The CVC/consulting on-ramps above are faster but lower-ceiling; the operator route is slower but higher-ceiling. Both are worth keeping open.

## What to build now, independent of the next job

These don't require a job change and compound over the full 10-year window:

- **Angel investing / scout programs** — lowest-commitment entry point. Healthcare-focused funds (Rock Health, .406 Ventures, Define Ventures, and similar) run scout networks; scouting while fully employed builds a track record and fund relationships without requiring a role change.
- **Public thought leadership** in digital phenotyping / digital biomarkers (writing, LinkedIn, conference talks) — the actual credential VCs check before inviting someone in as an EIR or advisor; independent of employer.
- **Advisory / board seats** at early-stage digital health startups, even unpaid — each one is a diligence rep and a network node.
