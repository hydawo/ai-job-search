## 2026-09-15 — The Knee That Knew: a torn ACL read against two years of passive wearable data and the clinical record
**Source:** `health_data_analytics`. Vault brief [`acl-injury-recovery.md`](../brainiac/writeups/health_data_analytics/acl-injury-recovery.md) (reviewed, ready to publish). Published 2026-09-12 at [hydawood.com/projects/acl-injury-recovery](https://hydawood.com/projects/acl-injury-recovery) with a full results page; on LinkedIn Projects since 2026-09-15 (see memory `project_linkedin_projects.md`).
**What:** Single-subject case study. A skiing ACL tear (Feb 2023) went undiagnosed for six months; two hands-on exams missed it and an MRI found it; reconstruction Sep 2023. The project reads the passive record from that whole period (iPhone gait metrics, Apple Watch, Oura from one month pre-op, a ski-tracking app, a gym log with left/right legs separate) against the clinical record (FHIR records from the Apple Health export plus 37 visit notes), with the clinical timeline as the reference rather than the calendar. Method highlights: alert rules referenced only to pre-injury percentiles and run on the same calendar dates of a healthy year as a control (composite alert fired day 15, zero false alarms in the control year, every alert off within two months); tear vs. surgery compared like-for-like as standardized effects against a written-down prior prediction, which failed (surgery was the larger perturbation on every comparable metric); recovery timed as days to re-enter the pre-injury interquartile band, giving a fixed order (double support at 59 days, walking speed at 199, first run 349, first ski day 812); a Fourier seasonal baseline fitted on three prior winters to separate season from lapsed PT (season explained 6% of a trough first blamed on winter); a setback recorded only in a PT note found in stair-descent speed and sleep fragmentation to the day. Negatives kept in: hop and range of motion have no sensor proxy, PT starting had no passive counterpart, strength gains never showed on the stairs, and the passive data said "injured, then recovered" exactly as the clinicians' hands did.
**Skills demonstrated:** Multi-source passive-data integration phased by a clinical timeline (the same join problem Beiwe/Forest solve for studies, with a clinical record as ground truth); honest detectability testing with a healthy-year control instead of pointing at a drop already known to be there; pre-registered-style prediction that was allowed to fail; season/confound separation; catching one's own wrong reading (the "winter" trough) by testing it; stating what the data could not see as prominently as what it could. This is the piece that best shows the gap between "wearables detect injury" marketing and what a passive record actually supports, which is exactly the trust/evaluation judgment the clinical-AI lane hires for.
**Draft bullet:** *"Ran a two-year single-subject study reading passive iPhone, Apple Watch, and Oura data against my own clinical record after an ACL tear and reconstruction: alert rules built only on pre-injury data flagged the tear by day 15 with zero false alarms in a control year, then went quiet within two months, while post-surgery recovery re-entered baseline in a fixed order and a setback recorded only in a physical-therapy note was dated to the day from stair-descent speed and sleep."*
**Needs verification before external use:** Numbers above are from the brief's "preserve exactly" list; keep to plain counts and days in letters (no effect sizes or p-values, per the standing no-stats-notation rule). Surgical detail stays at what the public post already states; the medication course is flagged in the brief as the author's own detail and is not to be added. Clinician and resort names are generalised everywhere. ## 2026-09-11 — health-agent: local-first Q&A over personal health data (open-source build, public since 2026-09-15)
**Source:** `../local_health_agent/` — public repo [github.com/hydawo/local-health-agent](https://github.com/hydawo/local-health-agent). README, THREAT_MODEL.md, ROADMAP.md, `background-and-differentiation.md`, and git history (first commit 2026-08-16, 24 commits through 2026-09-01) read directly 2026-09-11. Built with Claude Code (`local-health-agent-plan.md` is the Claude Code build plan). No `brainiac/writeups/` entry yet.
**What:** A single-user CLI (`health-agent`) that answers natural-language questions about the user's own health data, with the data and the model both staying on the machine. Ingests Apple Health exports (export.xml/zip), bloodwork PDFs via OCR, medical records, and personal notes into a SQLite index plus a file-based vector store; a local model (Ollama on loopback) reads the index and cites the file and page behind every number. v0.1 is feature-complete for its scope. Privacy is designed to be *falsifiable* rather than asserted: `health-agent offline-check` runs the whole pipeline (ingest, OCR, aggregation, lab trends, vector search, every agent tool) inside a kernel-level sandbox with networking denied and reports which enforcement applied; a test asserts the network surface is exactly two modules and counts the vendor SDK, so a cloud backend cannot ship data off-machine unnoticed. Cloud inference (Anthropic) exists as an explicit, consent-recorded opt-in, never a silent fallback. Accuracy is measured against a 20-question hand-verified eval set. A second phase added a medical-literature corpus with evidence-graded citations (meta-analysis/systematic review > RCT > observational > case report) and a guardrail that flags any general clinical claim made without a citation. Origin: prompted by ChatGPT Health (July 2026) as a demonstration that the architectural alternative, data staying on-device, is buildable and checkable.
**Skills demonstrated:** End-to-end system design for multi-source health data (structured HealthKit + unstructured PDFs/notes), provenance and reproducibility as product requirements (per-answer citations, versioned corpus), threat modeling with verifiable claims, evaluation rigor (hand-verified eval set, deterministic tool-layer tests), responsible-AI judgment (evidence grading, uncertainty framing, no-citation guardrail, explicit not-a-medical-device stance), and the same "research-infrastructure discipline applied to consumer health AI" narrative that runs through the Beiwe work. Directly relevant to any role touching human-in-the-loop AI, AI evaluation, or clinical AI trust (Wolters Kluwer's posting names all three).
**Draft bullet:** *"Built health-agent, an open-source local-first tool that answers questions about a person's own Apple Health, bloodwork, and medical-record data with the model and data staying on-device, citing the file and page behind every figure; its privacy claims are falsifiable by design (a sandboxed offline check and a test pinning the network surface), accuracy is measured against a hand-verified eval set, and literature answers carry evidence-graded citations."*
**Needs verification before external use:** "Built" is accurate here (Hassan's own repo, with Claude Code). Do not describe it as a product or as validated on real patients; the README calls it a proof of concept and not a medical device, and the eval set is 20 questions on synthetic fixtures. Confirm with Hassan whether he wants the ChatGPT Health origin mentioned externally (his own notes say to keep that contrast architectural and fair, not adversarial). Version and commit counts will drift; re-check the repo before quoting them.
